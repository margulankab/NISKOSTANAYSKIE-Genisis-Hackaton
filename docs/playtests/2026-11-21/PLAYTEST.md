# Memory Worlds playtest · 21 November 2026

**Verdict.** Memory Worlds has a cohesive visual identity and a satisfying premise. Walking back into a low-poly space with the original image and the day’s own song gives the diary a clear identity. The current build captures a setting and its broad tone more reliably than the event that made the day worth keeping. The joyful farm was recognizable, the cave felt a little eerie, and the quiet room was easy to read. Sadness was the weakest result. In both station visits, the most moving detail was the folded ticket in the input, and that detail never became the object or moment the player discovers.

The [original brief](../../../reference/document_en.md) asks the diary to preserve how a day *felt*, with text, photos, and the associated song shaping the result. It also asks for a recognizable visual system across different days. The low-poly materials, typography, palette, rules-based places, and consistent interactions support the second goal. The first needs more specific discoveries and endings, especially for entries with mixed or understated feelings. The [game implementation plan](../../memory_worlds_implementation_plan.md) also calls for meaningful objects, recognizable settings, and a closing moment tied to the place. The four generated worlds pass the basic walking loop. Their emotional payoffs vary much less than their prompts.

## Inputs and method

All entries and media used in this review are fictional. The diary uses an [original illustration](inputs/last-platform-illustration.png) in the photo slot and an [original 9-second audio file](inputs/last-platform-original.wav). The prompt worlds were built through **New game from a prompt** in the local private planner. The diary used **Create a memory**. The test browser used fresh profiles. Re-enter these prompts to regenerate the worlds, or import the diary ZIP through **Archive**.

- **Build memory, harvest.** “A joyful dawn at the farm, after the first harvest. I met Ada at the windmill. We raced past three haystacks and a red bench, then laughed by the well because the baskets had split.”
- **Build memory, station.** “A sad farewell at a train station at dusk in the rain. I met Noor by the ticket kiosk. She gave me the last ticket beside the street clock and walked out before I could say goodbye.”
- **Build memory, cavern.** “An eerie night in a cavern with four blue crystal clusters and two standing stones. I met Leena beside a lantern shrine. The lantern went out, but we could still hear it humming.”
- **Build world, library.** “A quiet library after the storm, at dawn. Two bookcases, a floor lamp, an armchair beside the window, and no tables. The rain has stopped and the first light reaches the returned books.”
- **Unsupported setting.** “A planetarium with an orrery and a slowly rotating ceiling of constellations.”
- **Diary, 18 October 2026.** “At dusk in the rain I waited with my sister at the train station. At the ticket kiosk she folded her last ticket into my coat pocket and asked me to keep it until she came home. The street clock kept ticking after the lights went out. I pressed the damp paper flat when I got home, and the apartment felt too quiet.”

Playwright drove player keyboard inputs in Edge 158.0.4370.33 against the current local checkout based on commit 798ea7f, including the existing working-tree changes. Every playable world reached both discoveries and its ending. The cavern was visited in reverse discovery order. Prompt Continue restored the ending and position. The diary reopened after the save warning, refreshed on the saved URL, and imported at spawn in a fresh profile. The diary export contained byte-identical input image and WAV. All four selected prompt soundtracks and the day audio advanced after gesture-started playback. The private playthroughs recorded zero external HTTP requests and zero page errors.

The mood judgments are one reviewer’s reading of fictional inputs. The audio assessment uses the selected tracks, composition characteristics, and verified browser playback. Hardware audibility, authored memory-owner feedback, physical touch, and live provider generation are outside this playtest.
