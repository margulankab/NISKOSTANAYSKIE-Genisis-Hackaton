## **`01`** `//` **`The Challenge`** 

```
Photos in a gallery preserve how a day looked, but not how it felt. Six months later we
scroll through the pictures and no longer remember the mood, the weather inside us, or the
song that was playing at the time.
```

```
Your task is to create a personal diary that preserves precisely the atmosphere of a day. A
person tells how their day went, attaches photos and a song, and the system turns all of it
into a beautiful memory (a post, a short video with music, or something entirely different)
and saves it in a separate folder for that day. Opening such an entry a month or a year later,
the person should feel that day all over again.
```

```
The invisible in this case is the feeling of the day. The visible is what your product turns it into.
```

## **`02`** `//` **`User Scenario`** 

- `├─` **`Entry.`** `At the end of the day, the user writes how their day went: free-form text, brief or detailed.` 

- `├─` **`Photos.`** `Attaches one or more photos from that day.` 

- `├─` **`Song.`** `Adds a song associated with the day: via a link to a music service or their own audio file.` 

- `├─` **`Design.`** `The system assembles a memory of the day from this in the format chosen by the team: a post, a carousel, a short video with music, an animated postcard, an interactive scene, or something of your own. Palette, texture, typography, composition, motion and caption are tuned to the mood of the day.` 

- `├─` **`Day folder.`** `The system comes up with a short title for the day based on the meaning of the entry, for example "An Autumn Evening with My Girlfriend by the Pond", and saves everything related to the day in a folder with that name: text, photos, song, the finished memory and the voice-over.` 

- `├─` **`Voice-over (optional).`** `The text of the entry can be read aloud in a voice matched to the atmosphere of the day: quiet and warm for a calm evening, lively and fast for an eventful day.`

## **`03`** `//` **`Example`** 

```
The user writes: "We walked in the park, everything was covered in yellow leaves, it was cold,
but we drank cocoa and talked for a long time." They attach three photos and a song by Frank
Sinatra. The result is a warm short video: the photos slowly replace one another in orange-ochre
tones, processed with soft brushstrokes in the spirit of the Impressionists and Claude Monet,
a gentle film grain runs across the frames, a handwritten caption appears, and the chosen song
plays underneath it all. The text can be listened to in the voice of a calm, warm narrator. The
day goes into a folder called "Autumn Park and Cocoa".
```

```
Another day: a rush to deliver a project, a night in the office, electronic music in the
headphones. The result comes out cold and focused: a blue-gray palette, a rigid grid, contrasting
typography, a sharp rhythm of frames and a geometric composition of lines and patches, like
Kandinsky. The folder is called "Night at the Office Before the Deadline".
```

```
Both days must still be recognizable as pages of the same diary.
```

```
The format is not limited to a post: it can be a short video with music, an animated
postcard, an interactive scene, a soundscape or anything else. The main goal is to convey
the atmosphere of the day as precisely as possible.
```

## **`04`** `//` **`A Unified Visual Language`** 

```
A diary is a series, not a collection of random pictures. All generations must follow common
principles that the team develops itself: the rules by which the mood of a day
is turned into form.
```

- `├─` **`Format.`** `The team chooses the format itself: a 1:1 or 4:5 post, a carousel, a vertical video of 15-60 seconds, an interactive scene or something of its own. Within one diary, the format must remain recognizable from day to day.` 

- `├─` **`Constant elements.`** `The grid, fonts, place for the date and caption, the way photos are embedded — whatever makes the feed cohesive.` 

- `├─` **`Variable elements.`** `Palette, photo treatment, texture, composition, rhythm, animation — whatever changes along with the mood, photos and music.` 

- `├─` **`Artistic techniques and references.`** `Describe which techniques and sources of inspiration your system draws on: for example, Kandinsky's composition for tense days, Claude Monet's brushwork and light for calm ones, film photography, collage, Japanese minimalist graphics. Specify which technique is triggered by which mood and why it conveys exactly that feeling.` 

- `├─` **`Explainable logic.`** `The team must be able to explain why a particular day looks the way it does. For example: a slow minor-key song gives more air and muted colors.`

- `├─` **`Titles and voice.`** `Folder titles are written in one tone and one length (3-7 words). The voice-over's voice, if there is one, is also chosen by the system's rules, not at random.` 

- `└─` **`The feed test.`** `5-7 different days side by side should look like the diary of one person, yet each day remains recognizably its own.` 

## **`05`** `//` **`What a Day's Memory Is Built From`** 

- `├─` **`Text:`** `mood, events, places, people, season and time of day.` 

- `├─` **`Photos:`** `dominant colors, light, subject, number of frames.` 

- `├─` **`Music:`** `tempo, energy, major or minor key, genre, era — through audio analysis or at least a description of the song (artist, genre, year). The song must influence the design, not just be attached to the result.` 

- `└─` **`Additional (optional and with the user's consent):`** `date, weather, geolocation.` 

## **`06`** `//` **`Freedom of Implementation`** 

```
The platform is the team's choice: a web app, a mobile app, a Telegram bot or another
format. You can use language models to analyze text and come up with titles, computer vision
for photos, audio analysis, image generation, speech synthesis for the voice-over,
generative graphics, WebGL and other tools.
```

```
Using AI is optional. AI by itself does not earn extra points. If you use generative models,
their output must still obey your visual system rather than being a random picture.
```

```
The output format is not limited: a post, video, animation, interactive web scene,
sound collage and any other solutions will do. All that matters is that the result can be opened
in the prototype and revisited later.
```

## **`07`** `//` **`Minimum Requirements`** 

- `├─ Input: text about the day, at least one photo and a song (link or file).` 

- `├─ Output: a designed memory of the day in a format of the team's choice (post, video, animation, interactive, etc.) that is built from the user's input rather than randomly picked from ready-made templates.` 

- `├─ Music noticeably influences the design and plays (or opens) when viewing the entry.` 

- `├─ Each day is stored in a separate folder with a short title that the system comes up with based on the entry.` 

- `├─ An archive of days where you can return to any past day.` 

- `├─ A unified visual system: the demonstration includes at least 3 days with different moods.` 

- `├─ The team's own contribution to the idea and implementation. Libraries, open-source and AI tools are allowed provided their use is disclosed.` 

- `└─ The ability to verify the prototype via instructions or a video demonstration.` 


## **`08`** `//` **`Advice for Participants: Start with a Favorite Day`** 

```
Choose one of the favorite days of your life and try to recreate it in the prototype
as accurately as possible. Write about it the way you would tell a close friend, take real
photos and the very song that was playing then.
```

- `├─ Be specific: weather, light, smells, sounds, people nearby, small details that stuck in your memory.` 

- `├─ Compare the result with your own feelings and refine the system until it resembles what you remember.` 

- `└─ Show this day in the demonstration. The goal is for the jury, who were never there, to feel the atmosphere of your day. If the viewer wants to be inside it, the product works.`
